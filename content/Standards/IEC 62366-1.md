**Medical devices — Part 1: Application of usability engineering to medical devices.** Edition 1.0 (2015) with Amendment 1 (2020); consolidated version available. Replaced IEC 62366:2007. European adoption EN 62366-1.

## Scope (paraphrased)

Specifies a process for manufacturers to analyse, specify, develop and evaluate the usability of a medical device as it relates to safety. The process is linked to risk management (ISO 14971) and covers correct use and *use errors*, but not deliberate abnormal use.

## What it gives the designer

- A **process**: use specification, hazard-related use scenarios, formative evaluations during design, a summative evaluation at the end, and a usability engineering file.
- The concept of **use error** — an action or omission that leads to a result different from what the manufacturer intended or the user expected — framed as a design problem, not user carelessness.
- No numeric limits: acceptance is argued through residual use-related risk.

Although written for medical devices, it is the most complete published process for *safety-related* usability and is often borrowed as a reference in machinery and industrial HMI projects.

## Notes

Metrics that follow from it: [[Use error rate]], [[Task success rate]] on hazard-related tasks, and qualitative observations of close calls and difficulties.
