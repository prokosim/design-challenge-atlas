## What it is

Representative users perform realistic tasks with a prototype or product while observers record what happens. *Formative* tests find problems during design; *summative* tests check at the end whether the design meets its goals (e.g. the summative evaluation required by [[IEC 62366-1]]).

## How it works

1. Define users, tasks and success criteria (often derived from a [[Hierarchical task analysis]]).
2. Run sessions (lab, simulator or field), usually recorded on video.
3. Measure effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction ([[ISO 9241-11]]).
4. Analyse errors and root causes; report (e.g. in [[ISO 25062]] format).

## Strengths

- Direct evidence of real behaviour, including errors designers did not anticipate.
- Produces the core usability KPIs.

## Limitations

- Needs a working prototype; results come late in the design process.
- Small samples; emergency scenarios are hard to simulate realistically.
- Error coding from video is manual and slow — see [[Automated use-error detection and classification]].
