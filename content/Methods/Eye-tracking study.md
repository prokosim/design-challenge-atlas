## What it is

Recording where people look, for how long and in which order, with a screen-based or wearable eye tracker, while they perform tasks.

## How it works

1. Define areas of interest (AOIs) — e.g. the emergency stop, a warning display.
2. Calibrate the tracker for each participant.
3. Record gaze during tasks; map gaze onto AOIs (for wearable trackers, onto reference images).
4. Compute metrics such as [[Time to first fixation]], fixation duration and dwell time.

## Strengths

- Objective evidence of visual attention and search — explains *why* a control is not found.

## Limitations

- Looking is not the same as seeing or understanding.
- Wearable tracking in dynamic conditions loses accuracy; AOI mapping is laborious.
- Pupil size as a workload measure is confounded by lighting ([[Peysakhovich et al 2015]]).
