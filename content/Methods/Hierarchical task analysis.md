## What it is

A structured breakdown of a task into goals, sub-goals and operations, with *plans* that say in which order and under which conditions they are carried out. Originally proposed by Annett & Duncan (1967).

## How it works

1. State the overall goal (e.g. *stop the machine safely in an emergency*).
2. Decompose it into sub-goals and operations until each step is observable.
3. Add plans (sequence, conditions).
4. Use the result to identify error opportunities, define test tasks and set success criteria.

## Strengths

- Cheap, early, needs no prototype.
- Basis for error analysis and for designing usability test tasks.

## Limitations

- Describes the ideal task; does not measure anything by itself.
- Quality depends on the analyst.
